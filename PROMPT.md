# Reproduce Ocean Shader Lab

Build a complete, polished standalone browser experiment called “Ocean Shader Lab — Between land and sea.” Use Vite 8.3.2, TypeScript 7.0.2 and Three.js 0.186.1 with @types/three 0.186.0. Use native HTML controls, custom GLSL, a single WebGL2 renderer and no React/backend. Pin dependencies and keep the lockfile. Node 26.7.0 or a supported even Node >=22.12 is required. Development runs at http://127.0.0.1:4173.

Visual target: a natural coastal view that aims toward photographic realism. A flat-shaded or visibly low-poly diorama is unacceptable. Fill the frame with clear turquoise water in front, pale sand and gently vegetated dunes behind, and irregular smooth rock outcrops at the right. Do not display a rectangular model boundary or cutaway. Use visible submerged rocks and a sandy floor, light refraction, depth absorption, multi-scale water normals, Fresnel reflections, restrained sun glints, fine irregular foam, moving caustic-like light on the floor, wet shore material transitions and natural shadows. Prioritize water and shore; marine animals are optional later. Use original procedural textures/geometry, no purchased assets or generation API. Treat near-photographic realism as a visual goal to inspect in real screenshots, not a claim justified by test counts.

World and geometry: seed 7. Keep a shared 129×129 Float32 central height map, x=-16..16 and z=-12..12, and a matching rock footprint mask. CPU bilinear heights and shader bilinear heights must agree at grid nodes. Extend the rendered terrain to 64×48 with 256×192 segments using the same clamped sampler; beyond sea z=12 extend the floor downward at .12 units per unit z. Positive z is sea. Do not use disconnected shoreline masks. Water spans 128×96, with Low/Balanced/High subdivisions 160/224/320 and proportional depth subdivisions. Smooth and deform merged IcosahedronGeometry detail 8 rocks with coherent noise, three reused geometries/materials, and original 512-pixel sand/stone albedo, bump and roughness maps. Build grass from instanced crossed curved tapered ribbons; no cone grass or dodecahedron boulders.

Camera: perspective FOV 49, look at [0,.35,-3.5]. Desktop position [4,3.5,11.5]; portrait [3,3.3,11]. Orbit 45°..83° from vertical with distance 8..23. Prevent underground views. Reset/resize must preserve the same orbit target and selected quality. Use procedural Three.js Sky daylight with sun direction shared by water and lighting; hemisphere illumination plus a directional light with 1024px soft static shadows. Update shadows only when needed. Use ACES tone mapping once and correct sRGB output. Sparse serif heading and quiet native toolbar; readable contrast over the light scene, labelled >=44px controls and visible keyboard focus.

Optical water: custom directional height waves with analytic gradients and depth attenuation. Three large wave bands displace geometry; additional fine normal ripples use pixel-footprint filtering to prevent distant aliasing. Capture the opaque coast into a linear color target and matching depth texture while water is hidden. Use a floating color target when supported, otherwise an 8-bit target. Refract screen-space floor/rock samples using the normal in camera space; reject bent samples that cross foreground geometry. Beer-Lambert depth absorption shifts clear shallows to blue/green deep water; Fresnel F0 .0204 mixes procedural sky and sun reflection. Use the depth target to preserve rocks emerging through the surface.

Performance: cache the stationary coast color/depth at drawing-buffer resolution until camera world/projection matrices, tide, sun, quality or buffer size changes. Render cached coast as the background with opaque coast objects temporarily hidden, then draw moving water. Always restore scene visibility, background, target and tone mapping in finally blocks. Ordinary wave animation must not redraw every rock/terrain object. Water caustics/foam move in the shader; tide/light invalidate the capture. A future moving underwater object must explicitly invalidate the stationary capture. Target resources are owned by the water factory and disposed exactly once. There is one simulation/render clock; no extra loop for effects.

Controls: Play/Pause, swell 0..1 (default .55), water level -.35..+.35 scene units (default 0), sun azimuth 0..360° (default 225), Auto/Low/Balanced/High, camera reset, fullscreen. On fullscreen rejection show a user-clickable standalone link without opening a popup automatically. Settings expand on demand.

Lifecycle: reduced-motion starts paused with a rendered still; Play animates. Hidden tabs cancel animation and do not advance simulation time. Resume uses delta zero initially and clamps later deltas to .05 seconds. Dispose is idempotent: cancel RAF, observers, input/message listeners owned by the controller, geometries/materials/textures/render targets, renderer lists and context. Unwind partially acquired resources when the first GPU draw throws. On persisted pageshow restore a disposed scene. Retry uses a fresh canvas and reapplies current tide, swell, sunlight and quality.

Auto starts Balanced, aims for 60 desktop/30 small-screen rendered frames per second and lowers profile after a sustained 3-second window above 1.35× its frame budget. Measure intervals between actual renders, preserve timing remainder, and never override manual quality. DPR caps are 1/1.5/2. Targets are goals, not guarantees. Identify software GPU, viewport emulation and physical device results separately.

Fallback: WebGL2 absence, shader failure or context loss yields an own-scene poster, readable message, Retry, GitHub source and full prompt links. Keep navigation usable and never leave a blank canvas. Preserve an earlier scene screenshot only in private comparison evidence, not as the final poster.

Embed contract: messages {channel:'ocean-demo',version:1,type:'ready'|'pause'|'dispose'}, boolean paused required on pause. Validate exact origin, source window, channel/version/type/payload. Production parent https://portfolio.muum.ai; explicit local development parent http://127.0.0.1:4321 must be absent from production JavaScript. Send ready after the first valid render. Combine host pause with user pause. Dispose does not reply. Escape from an iframe sends dispose to its trusted parent. Messages cannot set a URL, HTML or execute code.

Modules: scene/terrain.ts, rocks.ts, camera.ts, textures.ts; water/water.ts and vertex/fragment GLSL; runtime/frame-loop.ts, render-pacer.ts, quality.ts, demo.ts; native ui/controls.ts, bridge/messages.ts, main.ts and styles.css. Keep render resources and loop state separate.

Meaningful Vitest/Playwright validation: seeded terrain and CPU/texture agreement, loop pause/resume/hidden/permanent disposal, manual versus Auto quality, actual rendered pacing, origin/source rejection, actual GPU movement and deterministic repeat, two tide extremes, visible submerged red/blue floor at fixed time, <=4 draw calls per ordinary moving-water frame after caching, mobile controls, reduced motion, WebGL absence, shader/context failure, first-draw resource unwind, retry control state, persisted history restoration and fullscreen rejection. Capture/inspect desktop and mobile pixels and the old/new comparison. Measure actual renders, not callback counts. No test result proves photographic quality.

Final visual settings: constrain camera azimuth to -0.9…0.9 radians (polar 45…83 degrees, distance 8…23) so the eye stays above the sampled dunes and maximum tide. Read the current camera aspect on reset. Use coherent non-periodic sand-height noise and patch-based grass placement instead of regular bands. Domain-warp wave phases with matching analytical derivatives. Enable the installed Three Sky cloud uniforms and scale only its HDR radiance by 0.08 before final tone mapping; cache the sky with the coast. The optics fixture must call the actual cached water.render composite.
